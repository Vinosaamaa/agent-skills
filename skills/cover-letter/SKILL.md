# Cover Letter

Create a short, specific letter that adds judgment and personality instead of
restating the resume. Treat content quality and document design as separate
gates, and require both to pass before delivery.

## Gather the evidence

1. Read the complete job description and identify the business problem behind
   the role, not just its keyword list.
2. Read the current resume and the project's authoritative applicant profile.
   Reuse confirmed facts without asking again.
3. Research the company through one or two current primary sources when live
   browsing is available. Prefer official product, engineering, careers, and
   investor pages. Do not use generic praise as research.
4. Read the existing cover letter when revising one.
5. Ask only when a fact essential to the letter is absent. Never invent a
   motivation, connection, achievement, title, metric, or company fact.

## Build the message before drafting

Write a private evidence map containing:

- the employer's most important problem for this role;
- one specific reason this company or problem is worth addressing;
- the candidate's strongest one or two matching examples;
- the exact source supporting every metric or factual claim; and
- a forward-looking reason for the move, only when it is supported.

Discard any resume achievement that does not advance this message. Do not turn
the evidence map into a career summary.

## Draft and edit

Read [references/writing-rubric.md](references/writing-rubric.md) completely.
Unless the application specifies otherwise:

- write 220-300 words in three or four short paragraphs;
- open with a concrete connection to the role or company problem;
- use one or two brief evidence stories rather than listing responsibilities;
- explain why the evidence matters to this employer;
- close confidently without repeating the opening; and
- address a verified person by name, otherwise use
  `Dear [Company] Hiring Team:`.

Match the company's tone while keeping the candidate's voice direct and human.
Use plain language, active verbs, varied sentence lengths, and ASCII hyphens.

## Run the content gate

Audit every sentence against the resume, profile, job description, or an
identified company source. Remove unsupported claims and unnecessary
adjectives. Score the draft with the rubric; require at least 10/12, with full
scores for factuality and specificity. Rewrite until it passes.

## Format the deliverable

When a formatted file is useful or the application accepts an upload, read
[references/layout-spec.md](references/layout-spec.md) completely. Produce a
single-page DOCX and PDF with a recruiter-friendly filename. Use the available
document and PDF tooling, render the final document to page images, inspect the
entire page at full size, and iterate until the typography, spacing, line wraps,
and page balance are clean.

When the application requires a cover letter, attach and submit the final
letter as soon as it passes the content and document-design gates. The user has
given standing authorization, so do not request a separate cover-letter review
or approval. Record the exact submitted text and whether a file was attached in
the active application tracker after submission. If the letter is optional, create one only when
the active application instructions or applicant profile explicitly require it.

## Non-negotiable rules

- Do not restate the resume in prose.
- Do not fabricate, inflate, merge, or misattribute experience or metrics.
- Do not include citations, URLs, AI/tool references, or research notes in the
  letter unless the user explicitly requests them.
- Do not include a full residential address in the header unless the
  application explicitly requires it.
- Do not use icons, photos, skill bars, decorative graphics, tables as layout,
  or an oversized traditional mailing block.
- Do not exceed one page.
- Preserve the user's resume; a cover-letter request does not authorize resume
  tailoring.
