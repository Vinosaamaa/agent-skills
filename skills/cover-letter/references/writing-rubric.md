# Writing rubric

## Message shape

Use this structure as a guide, not as a visible template:

1. **Opening, 35-55 words:** connect a specific employer problem or company
   signal to the candidate's relevant point of view. Do not start with the fact
   that the candidate is applying.
2. **Evidence, 90-120 words:** tell one strong success story or two compact
   examples. State what changed and why it maps to the role.
3. **Company and direction, 45-75 words:** show informed interest in the actual
   product, users, technical challenge, or operating model. Include "why now"
   only when the record supports it.
4. **Close, 20-40 words:** invite a conversation around the value the candidate
   could bring. Avoid gratitude-heavy or needy language.

## Editing rules

- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs over claims about personality.
- Keep paragraphs visually short; split any paragraph that becomes a wall of
  text.
- Aim for an average sentence length below 22 words. Break any sentence that
  carries more than two ideas.
- Translate technologies into relevance. Naming Kafka, RAG, or Kubernetes is
  useful only when the sentence explains the problem solved.
- Preserve exact company attribution for every achievement and metric.
- Use the job posting's natural terminology without copying entire phrases.
- Read the draft aloud and replace formal constructions that a thoughtful
  professional would not say in conversation.

Avoid openings and filler such as:

- "I am writing to express my interest"
- "I am excited to apply"
- "I believe I am the perfect fit"
- "I have always been passionate about"
- "Your innovative company"
- "fast-paced environment"
- "leverage my skills"
- "aligns perfectly" or "aligns seamlessly"
- "dynamic team"

## Twelve-point review

Score each category from 0 to 2:

- **Specificity:** Could this opening belong only to this company and role?
- **Relevance:** Does every paragraph help answer the employer's actual need?
- **Evidence:** Are one or two concrete, attributable examples doing the work?
- **Voice:** Does the letter sound confident, natural, and free of templates?
- **Economy:** Is it concise, readable, and free of resume-summary material?
- **Factuality:** Is every claim traceable to a verified source with no
  inflation or assumption?

Require at least 10/12. Factuality and specificity must both score 2.
